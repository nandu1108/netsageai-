VLAN Concepts and Troubleshooting

A VLAN (Virtual Local Area Network) logically segments a physical network into
separate broadcast domains. Devices in different VLANs cannot communicate
without a Layer 3 device (router or Layer 3 switch) performing inter-VLAN routing.

Common VLAN misconfigurations:

1. Missing VLAN on trunk ports: When a VLAN is created on a switch but not
   included in the "switchport trunk allowed vlan" list on trunk links between
   switches, devices in that VLAN cannot reach resources on other switches even
   though the VLAN itself exists locally. Symptom: intermittent or total
   connectivity failure for devices in that VLAN when trying to reach devices
   on a different switch.

2. VLAN not assigned to access port: If an access port is left in the default
   VLAN (often VLAN 1) instead of being assigned to the intended VLAN, the
   device will be logically isolated in the wrong broadcast domain.

3. Native VLAN mismatch: If trunk ports on either end of a link have different
   native VLANs configured, this causes VLAN leaking and unpredictable
   forwarding behavior. Native VLAN mismatches often show up in CDP warnings.

4. VLAN database not propagated (VTP issues): In VTP domains, if a switch is in
   the wrong VTP mode or domain, VLAN changes made on one switch may not
   propagate correctly to others.

Relevant commands:
- show vlan brief          -- lists VLANs and their assigned access ports
- show interfaces trunk    -- shows trunk ports and their allowed VLAN lists
- show vtp status          -- shows VTP domain and mode

Fix pattern: If a VLAN is missing from the trunk allowed list, add it with:
switchport trunk allowed vlan add <vlan_id>
